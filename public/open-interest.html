📊 Open Interest Intelligence
Is money entering or leaving futures positions, where is it happening, and does OI confirm or contradict price?

Open interest is the number of futures positions still open. Rising OI means new positions are being opened; falling OI means positions are being closed or liquidated. Read against price, the change describes how the market is positioning — it is an interpretation of positioning, never a trade signal.

        ℹ️ How this page works
        1. One server contract. The page reads /api/open-interest and /api/open-interest/:symbol. The server asks public, keyless futures endpoints — Binance USDⓈ-M first, then Bybit, OKX and Bitget — and the first venue that lists an asset serves its whole row. Venues are never summed for one asset.
        2. OI change is measured in coins, not dollars, so a price move alone never reads as leverage entering. Price comes from the dashboard’s shared Binance spot candles.
        3. Missing is missing. A venue that publishes no history (Bitget) or an asset no venue lists shows —, never 0%. Totals and breadth say how many assets they cover.
        4. Confluence reuses the screeners. Directional Bias and Local Extremes come from the same cached scan those pages use, on the Confluence timeframe you pick.
        5. Spikes compare the latest 1H OI change with the asset’s own recent 1H changes (z-score ≥ 2.5 and at least 1%).
        • Positioning, not signals. Nothing here means BUY or SELL.
        • Universe: the Open Interest column in Settings → Screeners.
        • AI use: No. Every label comes from fixed rules.
