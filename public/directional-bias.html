⚡ Directional Bias
Where trend and momentum are leaning across the shared 25-token universe — direction, not an entry

Directional Bias measures where trend and momentum are currently leaning. It does not represent an automatic trade entry.

The score counts how many of six checks — RSI, MACD, VWAP, volume, EMA20/50 structure and price against EMA200 — agree. It is confidence, not desirability: a BEARISH 83 is exactly as well evidenced as a BULLISH 83. For where price sits inside that move, see Local Extremes.

ℹ️ How this page works
  1. Choose a timeframe and rescan. Every row is scored on closed candles from the shared token universe, and the same cached scan feeds Local Extremes — the two pages always describe the same candles.
  2. Read the bias, then the score. BULLISH / BEARISH / NEUTRAL says which way the environment leans; the score says how much of the six-check evidence agrees.
  3. Use the component columns to see why. EMA structure, VWAP, MACD and volume are shown individually, so a row carried by one strong check is distinguishable from one where everything agrees.
  4. Check freshness before trusting a row. FRESH, STALE and UNAVAILABLE come from the shared freshness rules; STALE means the feed or the cache has stopped advancing, so the numbers are real but not now.
  • Bias is not an entry. BULLISH 67 means four of six checks lean bullish, not “go long now”. LONG / SHORT stay reserved for pages that publish an actual setup with an entry, stop, and target, such as the Decision Engine.
  • Direction is not location. A bullish bias says nothing about whether price is already stretched; that is what Local Extremes answers, and My Trades is where the two are read together.
  • AI use: No. Every score comes from fixed, inspectable indicator rules; this page does not call an AI model.
  • Probability, not certainty. A 100 score means all configured evidence agrees, not that price must follow.
